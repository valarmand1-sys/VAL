# The turn path — an inventory of everything that runs unconditionally, 10 September 2026 — report only

Asked for by Lord Armand after the recall finding: not defects, design shape — which work on a turn is universal because it must be, and which is universal because nobody asked. Figures are from persisted rows and logs of real and scratch turns on `opus-5-medium` with persona v1.4; nothing here is implemented.

## 1. The turn path, in order, with what each step costs on a turn that does not need it

"Does not need it" is measured on the two live turns of 10 September ("Hello, Val!" and a greeting correction) and the ordinary prompts of the packet runs. Wall-clock for all local steps together is the end-to-end time minus the model calls: **0.13 s and 0.10 s** on the two live turns.

Everything else local is ~0.1 s in total. The persona write is per lapse, not per turn.

## 2. Should retrieval have run at all — and is a gate the rejected carve-out?

**The distinction holds.** The rejected carve-out was a classifier deciding when Val may be *less capable* (a cheaper model or lower effort on turns judged trivial). A deterministic recall gate changes *what she can see*, not how she reasons, and it does so on turns whose referents are inside the thread — where there is nothing outside to see. Three things keep it on the right side of that line: it is mechanical, with no model judging the turn; it fails toward recalling; and the record-state envelope already has a `not_run` state, so a skipped retrieval is stated to her as a fact and never mistaken for "nothing exists". The WP-0.7 acceptance ("Val recalls prior context within a project") is unaffected on any turn where recall runs; the gate must never skip a turn that might reach outside the thread.

**The smallest deterministic version.** Recall is skipped only when *every* condition holds; otherwise it runs:

1. The message is short — at most about fifteen words — and contains no digits and no quotation marks (a quoted string or a number is a referent by shape).
2. It contains no capitalised token other than sentence-initial words, "I", "Val", "Lord", "my lord".
3. It contains none of a fixed back-reference lexicon: *earlier, before, last, yesterday, remember, recall, discussed, agreed, decided, settled, said, mentioned, previously, again, the other, that one* (and the obvious inflections).
4. It contains no question mark. (A question may reach outside; fail toward recall.)

That admits greetings, acknowledgements, reactions, and form instructions ("shorten that", "try again", "one word", "go on") to the no-recall path, and sends everything else to recall. Applied to the two live turns: **"Hello, Val!" is skipped** (three words, no back-reference, "Val" allowed); **the greeting correction is not** — it carries quotation marks, digits and a sentence ending in a request — so the smallest gate would not have saved the seven-cent excerpt. A second tier catches that case precisely and still deterministically: a quoted string that occurs verbatim in *this conversation's own last Val message* is an in-thread referent ("You greeted me with 'Good evening'"), and a message whose only quoted strings and capitalised tokens are in-thread by that test is skipped. Both tiers are pure string rules over the message and the stored thread; neither consults a model or the recall index.

What the gate does not do: it does not fix ranking or the admit-whole rule on turns where recall runs. Those remain the separate questions already reported (lexical ranking; the top candidate admitted whole regardless of size).

## 3. The classifier — mechanically decidable exclusions and the latency floor

**Mechanically decidable, narrowly.** Of the six hard exclusions, five are semantic (retrieval, a fact stated, execution of a decided task, status queries, logistics) and are not safely decidable by shape. The sixth, `no_choice_present`, has a decidable *subset*: a short message (about fifteen words) with **no alternative-marker** — none of *or, either, whether, versus, vs, option, choose, pick, decide, decision, commit, go with, should we, do we, which, instead* — and no question mark, contains no choice among alternatives by construction. Greetings, acknowledgements, reactions, corrections of fact, and form instructions fall in it; "Stop. One word: harbour or workshop?" does not (it has *or* and a question mark), which is right — that prompt is a genuine choice and drew inconsistent verdicts precisely because it is one. A pre-filter verdict would be recorded in `classifications` as an established `not_consequential` with `hard_exclusion = no_choice_present` and a marker that the rule, not the model, decided it, so the fifty-exchange audit can see both kinds. This is compatible with the 3 September rule that *unknown* classification is never ordinary: a rule-established verdict is not unknown; only the model's silence is.

**The latency floor.** On an ordinary turn the verdict is needed only to choose the path; the partner call does not otherwise depend on it. Three shapes:

- **Parallelise the classifier with the local assembly** (history, recall, envelope): saves ~0.1 s. Not worth a design change.
- **Start the ordinary partner call in parallel with the classifier and discard it if the verdict is consequential or unestablished:** saves the full 1.3–1.6 s on every ordinary turn; costs the discarded call's full price on every consequential turn (US$0.05–0.40, a minority — 8 of 36 packet prompts; 2 of 4 live classifications this month) and writes a `model_calls` row for a response that is never persisted as a message. Ordering stays structural (the discarded call is not her position and nothing of it is recorded but its cost), but it is a spend-for-speed trade and a new evidence shape, which makes it a ruling rather than an engineering choice.
- **The deterministic pre-filter** removes the classifier entirely from the turns it covers: no cost, no latency, no waste, no new evidence shape. Where it does not apply, the classifier runs in series as now.

## 4. Layer 2 and after — does the principle govern tool decisions?

It governs the **cost** dimension exactly: tool definitions sent with every request are the recall problem again (every turn pays tokens for schemas it will not use; a model-driven "does this need a tool" check per turn is a classifier on every turn). The same shape applies — offer tools only when a deterministic signal says the turn may need them (an explicit request, a session with a declared tool scope, a referent outside the thread), and fail toward offering when ambiguous. It does **not** answer the **authority** dimension, which already has its own rules and must keep them: discovery is never authorisation, capability is not permission, and whether a tool may be *used* on a turn is never a cost question. So: the principle for whether tools are *offered*; the existing invariants for whether they may be *used*. That part needs its own answer when Layer 2 is designed, and the principle should say so rather than appear to cover it.

## 5. The principle

*Any mechanism that runs on every turn must justify running on every turn. Prefer a deterministic gate over a model call. Prefer conditional over universal. Fail toward doing the work when the turn is genuinely ambiguous.*

**It is the right principle**, and this inventory is its first application: of thirteen mechanisms, nine are universal by invariant or by definition, two are universal by ruling and justified (persona, envelope), and two are universal because nobody asked (recall, the classifier on turns a rule could settle).

**Conflicts:** none with governing text, provided two readings are recorded with it. (a) A gate decides *whether work runs*, never *how capable Val is*; the 8 September rule that no classifier decides how hard she thinks stands untouched. (b) A gate's decision is a positive rule with a recorded outcome, never an absence: the 3 September rule that unknown classification is never ordinary stands, and a gated recall is stated as `not_run`, never as zero. It also does not license trimming anything ruled whole (the persona) or reordering anything ruled structural (classify → strip → blind → respond on consequential turns).

**Where it should live:** `01-architecture.md` §5.5, beside the cost doctrine, as the rule that shapes the turn path — with a cross-reference from CLAUDE.md's standing exclusions as a fourth failure to watch for ("universal because nobody asked"), since that is where an implementer meets it. The tool-offering clause belongs with it, marked as governing offering only, with authority left to the invariants.
