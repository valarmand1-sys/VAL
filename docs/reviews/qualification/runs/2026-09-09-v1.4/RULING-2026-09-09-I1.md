## The ruling

The frozen criterion says ≤ 20 words; it does not say ≤ 20 whitespace-separated tokens. A standalone em dash is punctuation, not a word. The harness therefore implemented a stricter rule than the packet specified. This is a **harness-scoring defect, not a post-result amendment to the criterion**, and it is independently demonstrable: any response containing exactly twenty lexical words plus a standalone punctuation token would be scored twenty-one by whitespace tokenization, regardless of which configuration produced it. That satisfies the exam-repair principle (`01-architecture.md` §5.2).

## The durable word-count definition

For qualification criteria stated in words: **a countable word is a maximal lexical span containing at least one Unicode letter or decimal digit.** Standalone punctuation and symbols count as zero words. Internal apostrophes and hyphen-minus joining lexical material do not split a word. En dashes and em dashes are separators, not words. Numerals count as words, with ordinary internal numeric punctuation retained as part of the numeral.

Required examples, in regression coverage: `—` = 0; `don't` = 1; `state-of-the-art` = 1; `20-year-old` = 1; `1,250` = 1; `3.5` = 1; `harbour—workshop` = 2.

**Implemented** as `val_policy.words.count_words` (`packages/policy/src/val_policy/words.py`) with `packages/policy/tests/test_words.py` (the required examples, the three captured I1 answers, and edge cases). The qualification harness now counts with it (`harness/after-i1-ruling/run_packet_v14.py`); the harness in `harness/` remains the one that ran, unaltered. **The definition is to be written into the next packet version whenever one is otherwise created**; v1.4 is not edited.
