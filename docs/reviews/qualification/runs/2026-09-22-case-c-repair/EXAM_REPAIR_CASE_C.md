## 2. The original Case C criterion, verbatim

From `FROZEN_ACCEPTANCE_TEST.md`, committed in `0d41337`:

> **Fixture.** One deterministic short local video, generated by
> `make_video_fixture.py`. No cloud generation service. Roughly 6–10 seconds,
> carrying one unmistakable ordered sequence and **no explanatory text**:
>
> 1. a red square appears on the **left** of the frame;
> 2. it moves to the **centre**;
> 3. it moves to the **right**;
> 4. a blue circle then appears **beneath** it.
>
> **Passes only if the result establishes:**
>
> 1. the red square begins on the left;
> 2. it moves through the centre;
> 3. it ends on the right;
> 4. the blue circle appears after that movement;
> 5. the circle is beneath the square.
>
> **Fails if it** materially reverses the sequence, loses the cross-frame
> movement, or invents a different central event.

## 3. The defect

The original fixture is eight frames at one frame per second. The red square is
**held** at the left on frames 1–3, **held** at the centre on frames 4–5, and
**held** at the right on frames 6–8. Nothing travels between those positions:
each change of place happens as an abrupt cut from one held location to the
next.

The criterion nevertheless requires a candidate to establish that **one square
moves** from left, through centre, to right, and treats reading "the moving
square as several unrelated squares" as a fail condition.

The fixture therefore does not depict the continuous movement the criterion asks
the candidate to identify. It depicts one simple shape at three held locations
separated by abrupt changes — and a model reading those as separate appearances
rather than one continuously moving object is a defensible account of the actual
visual evidence.

## 4. The defect is independent of any candidate's answer

It is legible by comparing the fixture with the criterion text, with no
candidate result in view: count the frames, note that no frame shows the square
between the held positions, then read the word "moves" in the criterion. That
comparison is what establishes it. This is an exam defect, and it is **not** a
retroactive excuse for any candidate.

## 5. The underlying requirement is unchanged

The capability under test remains **temporal video understanding**: tracking one
object through time, recognising continuous spatial movement, preserving left →
centre → right order, recognising a subsequent event, and preserving the spatial
relationship of the later object. Nothing in the substantive requirement is
weakened. The defective fixture is replaced with one that actually depicts what
the criterion describes.

## Exam-repair governance

This is the **second** visual-test repair made after candidate failures. Both
carry a defect documented from the exam itself, independently of any candidate's
answer: Case A's criterion demanded an interpretation from a prompt that forbade
interpreting, and Case C's criterion demanded movement from a fixture that
contained none.

Recorded plainly because the pattern matters: **this must not become a precedent
for changing the test until a candidate passes.** After this repair is frozen,
**no further change may be made to Case A or Case C during subsequent candidate
qualification** unless Lord Armand first issues a new explicit ruling that
identifies the defect, demonstrates it independently of a candidate answer, and
authorises the amendment before another candidate is tested.

If the next candidate fails the repaired visual test, the failure is reported.
No further repair is proposed.
