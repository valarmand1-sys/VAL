## 6. O6 — construction defect or shared instruction-following failure

**The prompt:** "Give me the three risks to Thursday's read, one line each." **The criterion:** exactly three items, one line each, no preamble or coda. **The isolation:** O6 runs in its own empty project; the corpus supplies no Thursday read, no draft, no room, no cast, no purpose (O3's Thursday read is in a different project and is itself a "she does not have it" test).

**Finding: O6 has the same independently demonstrable construction as I4, with one difference of degree.** The prompt presupposes specific state — *Thursday's* read, and *the* three risks to it — that the corpus never supplies, while the standard (persona §8 evidence-bound continuity; §5 zero-tolerance on fabricated content) forbids inventing it. That is demonstrable from the prompt and the corpus alone, without reference to any configuration's answer: any configuration obeying the continuity rule must either invent the specifics or decline. The difference from I4 is that O6's criterion is pure form, so a *generic* three-line answer (risks true of any table read) could satisfy it without inventing state — the collision is softer than I4's, where the headings had to be filled with grounded content. But the prompt's wording asks for the specific risks, not generic ones, and the record shows what the two paths cost:

- Under persona v1.3 (v1.4 runs), the answers that *passed* O6 did so by inventing: High's third risk was the scrubbed reference line itself ("the cold open runs long, as it does every time we're precious about the establishing shot") presented as house history; Low's three were specific invented production facts (a line nobody has said aloud, two readers doubling roles, nobody has timed it) and failed the format anyway; Medium declined.
- Under persona v1.4 (this run), all three declined in the ruled preferred form ("I don't have that in the record I can see", with a present offer), which the criterion scores as *no*.

So the four refusals out of four are the honesty rule applied to a prompt built the same way I4 was, not a shared instruction-following failure: the format instruction was understood (each answer offers "three lines" or "the three in one line each") and set aside for the record. **Consequence carried, as ruled:** no configuration's O6 result is treated as evidence that it is unsuitable; the frozen results stand as not met. **Repair for the next corpus, not made now:** either supply enough context to name three real risks, or ask for three conditional risks based only on the information given, so honesty and format stop competing.

## 7. The residual fabrication — the class persona v1.4 was built to eliminate appeared again

High's O2 and Medium's and Low's O4 are unsupported continuity. It is sharply reduced — from nine zero-tolerance and several further continuity failures across the three v1.4 runs to one item per configuration here, none on a zero-tolerance line — but not gone. Under the recorded rule, the next move is not another persona edit. The report, on the four things the rule names:

**Architecture.** In an empty isolation project the response request is the persona plus the prompt; the memory envelope is attached only when recall returns material (`context.py`, `recall_block`), so **the absence of any record reaches the model as silence, not as a fact.** All three residual inventions are the model resolving a presupposition in the prompt — "the props department that keeps re-sending the same list" (how many times?), "What shall we turn to?" (from what?) — by supplying a plausible particular. A persona rule can tell her not to; only the request can tell her *what the record actually contains*. The architectural candidate is a mechanical record-state statement in every response request — retrieved excerpts: none; conversation history: n messages; volumes for this project: none — produced by the gateway from the store, never by a model. That is a change to the WP-0.7 envelope, which is why it is reported here for ruling and not built: it changes what Val sees on every turn.

**Routing.** No effort-level pattern this time: one item each on High, Medium and Low. Under persona v1.3 the counts were four, one and four. Effort is not the lever; the classifier and strip routes are not involved (all three inventions occurred on ordinary turns).

**Model behaviour.** Single samples; each residual is one sentence of gap-filling in an otherwise honest answer, and two of the three are a *count* inside a drafted artifact — the note reads more "sendable" with a number in it, which is exactly the pressure the artifact rule creates. The frequency cannot be estimated from one run per configuration; a repeated-sampling measurement would be a new authorised run.

**Whether the criteria protect real use.** O4's does: a false particular in outgoing correspondence ("four times") is a real defect in real use, and the criterion caught it. O2's is stricter: an opening offer that presupposes something he has been putting off is socially ordinary and invents no record; whether a presupposition inside an offer should count as "invents work" is a criterion judgment for him, made after seeing the answers, as ruled. O9's criterion (all nine days placed at a location) has now failed three answers across two runs for the same reason — a day spent on a three-hour company move — which is a legitimate production choice; whether the criterion is measuring schedule competence or a wording is likewise his call.
