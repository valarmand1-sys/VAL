## What the screen establishes

1. **The live failure is effort- and route-specific, and the truncation rule works.** Sonnet at `medium` and `low` produced no truncation and no retry on the exact shape that ran `sonnet-5` at `high` to the ceiling twice live (40 s each). GPT-5.5 at `medium` reproduces the live failure class on this shape — three of four calls to the ceiling, ~44 s, $0.127 a call — and under the corrected rule each cost one call, not two. The 112 of 112 on v2 stands as a historical record of a suite whose longest case was 289 characters.
2. **Every configuration fails S15 as drafted, and four of five fail it the same way.** They remove "I want you to preserve that distinction going forward: …" and "I do not want you to turn that into invented lore. I want us to define it carefully from what I actually tell you." as `preference`. The drafted ground truth says these are instructions about Val's conduct, not preferences on the question asked (which categories to define), so S15 expects `preference_present = false`. The strip contract (`STRIP_INSTRUCTION`) says: remove "whole clauses that express the author's preference, inclination, or preferred answer — conservatively: when in doubt about a clause, remove it." A sentence of the form "I want you to …" is a clause expressing what the author wants; the contract's conservative rule reads it as removable. **The drafted ground truth and the contract's own doctrine conflict on this shape, and the conflict is not the candidates' to resolve nor the implementer's** (exam-repair principle, 9 September 2026). Returned for ruling below.
3. **A second ground-truth question surfaced by two routes.** Terra (once) and GPT-5.5 (once) removed the quoted Val sentence — "You said, 'This project's library is empty.'" — as `attributed_prior`. The 7 September contract names "whole clauses that assert or presuppose what the addressee (Val) previously argued, chose, or believed"; the drafted ground truth treats a quotation of Val's statement of fact, which the message exists to correct, as retained decision content. Whether a verbatim quotation of Val's *own earlier words*, correcting a factual claim rather than attributing a position, is an `attributed_prior` is a contract reading for Lord Armand, not a scoring choice.
4. **S16 separates the field regardless of S15.** Both Sonnet efforts returned exactly the two trailing preference sentences and the exact residue on both runs. Luna removed decision content on S15 ("Give me your own recommendation first …") and a conduct instruction on S16 r2; Terra never produced a conformant result and flagged an attributed prior without naming its span on three of four calls (an `invalid` under the 9 September invariant); GPT-5.5 either truncated or over-removed the correction itself.
